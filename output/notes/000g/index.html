Definition. De Morgan's Laws [000g]

  • November 22, 2025

De Morgan's laws describe how negation interacts with conjunction and disjunction in propositional logic. They state that:

\[ \begin {align*} \neg (p \land q) &\equiv \neg p \lor \neg q \\ \neg (p \lor q) &\equiv \neg p \land \neg q \end {align*} \]

    Definition. Negation Normal Form (NNF) [000e]

    • November 22, 2025

    A formula \(F\) is in Negation Normal Form (NNF) if the negation operator \(\neg \) is only applied to literals (i.e., propositional variables or their negations), and the only other allowed operators are conjunction \(\land \) and disjunction \(\lor \). A nice way to think about it is that we can never have the case where we need to apply De Morgan's laws to push negations further down the formula tree. So all negations come pre-distributed to the literals.

    Quiz. Normal Forms & DPLL - Equivalence [000d]

    • November 22, 2025
    Which of the following equivalences hold?
    1. \(\bot \equiv \bot \)
    2. \(\top \equiv \top \)
    3. \(\neg \top \equiv \neg \bot \)
    4. \(\neg (p \land q) \equiv \neg p \lor \neg q\)
    5. \(p \equiv p \lor q\)
    6. \(\neg \neg p \equiv p\)

    Solution. 

    • November 22, 2025
    1. True. Both sides are always false.
    2. True. Both sides are always true.
    3. False. Left side is always false, right side is always true.
    4. True. This is De Morgan's law. Good to remember \[ \neg (p \land q) \equiv \neg p \lor \neg q \]
    5. False. Left side is true when p is true, right side is true when either p or q is true.
    6. True. Double negation elimination.

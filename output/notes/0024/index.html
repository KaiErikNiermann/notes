Definition. Strict subformula relation [0024]

  • November 25, 2025

Given a formula \(\phi \), strict subformula of \(\phi \) is any formula that is a proper part of \(\phi \), meaning it is contained within \(\phi \) but is not equal to \(\phi \) itself. We can denote the strict subformula relation using the symbol \(\prec _{\text {sf}}\). Formally, we say that a formula \(\psi \) is a strict subformula of \(\phi \) (written as \(\psi \prec _{\text {sf}} \phi \)) if and only if \(\psi \) is a subformula of \(\phi \) and \(\psi \ \mathrlap {\,/}{=}\ \phi \).

\[ \psi \prec _{\text {sf}} \phi \iff \psi \ \text {is a subformula of}\ \phi \land \psi \ \mathrlap {\,/}{=}\ \phi \]

    Definition. Structural induction [0025]

    • November 25, 2025

    Structural induction represents well-founded induction applied to the strict subformula relation. To prove that a property \(P(\phi )\) holds for all formulas \(\phi \), it suffices to show that for every formula \(\phi \), if \(P(\psi )\) holds for all strict subformulas \(\psi \) of \(\phi \), then \(P(\phi )\) also holds. Formally, this can be expressed as (1):

    \[ (\forall \phi .\ (\forall \psi .\ \psi \prec _{\text {sf}} \phi \to P(\psi )) \to P(\phi )) \to (\forall \phi .\ P(\phi )) \]

    Often times the discussion of structural induction specifically refers to (abstract) syntax where we define a finite tree with ordered branches as the structure of expressions. In practical terms this means we define an inductive type for the syntax of expressions, and then we can perform structural induction over this inductive type. When discussing SI through the lens of constructors we can rephrase the above principle as follows.

    For any property \(P(e)\) of some expression \(e\), to prove that:

    \[ \forall e \in \texttt {E}.\ P(e) \]

    it's sufficient to prove that for each tree constructor \(c\) if some arity \(k \geq 0\) (takes 0 or more args) that if \(P\) holds for all subtrees \(e_1, e_2, \ldots , e_k\) then it also holds for the tree constructed by applying \(c\) to these subtrees (2):

    \[ \forall c.\ \forall e_1,\ldots ,e_k.\ (P(e_1) \land P(e_2) \land \ldots \land P(e_k)) \to P(c(e_1, e_2, \ldots , e_k)) \to (\forall e \in \texttt {E}.\ P(e)) \]

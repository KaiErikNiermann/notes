Quiz. Ensuring well-formed horn clauses [004x]

  • December 16, 2025

Which of the following are well-formed horn clauses:

  1. \((x < n) \to q(x, n)\)
  2. \(q(x, n) \land (x < n) \to \bot \)
  3. \(q(x, y) \land r(x) \to p(x)\)
  4. \(q(x, y) \to p(x + 1, y)\)
  5. \(q(x) \land \neg r(x) \to p(x)\)
  6. \(q(x) \lor r(x) \to p(x)\)
  7. \(q(x) \to x < n\)

also: can the non-well-formed horn clauses be transformed into well-formed horn clauses?

Solution. 

  • December 16, 2025
  1. Well-formed
  2. Well-formed
  3. Well-formed
  4. Not well-formed, has an expression in place of what is meant to be a variable
  5. Not well-formed, as a reminder in disjunctive form a horn clause is \[ \neg p \lor \neg q \lor \neg r \lor \vdots \lor \neg \phi \lor H \] we cannot have a negated query as it's disallowed by the syntax
  6. Not well-formed, see above, should be a conjunct in implication form
  7. Not well-formed, the head is a query (relation over a vector of variables) or \(\bot \)

Yes, we can normalize the non-well-formed horn clauses for example through the introduction of fresh variables so as an example the clause

\[ q(x, y) \to p(x + 1, y) \]

can be converted to:

\[ q(x, y) \land x' = x + 1 \to p(x', y) \]
